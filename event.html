The flow of the night

From aarti to the last taali

We've built the night the way a Garba night is meant to move — slow and devotional first, then faster, louder and completely unstoppable.

6:30 PM

Entry & Welcome

Gates open, tilak & welcome, photo corner with traditional toran backdrops, food stalls live.

7:30 PM

Aarti & Maa Ambe Sthapana

Traditional garbo lighting and aarti. Everything begins with Her blessing.

8:00 PM

Traditional Rounds

Do-taali, traan-taali, Hinch and classic Gujarati folk taal with live dhol and orchestra.

10:00 PM

Raas & Sanedo Peak

Dandiya raas circles, Sanedo, Titodo and full-throttle high-energy taal until the dhol gives up.

Questions

Frequently Asked

When exactly is the event?

The date is currently tentative and will fall before Paryushan. We're finalising the venue calendar now — register your interest and you'll be the first to receive the confirmed date.

Why hold a Garba before Paryushan?

Many of our founders and a large part of our community observe Paryushan. Holding our debut Garba beforehand means nobody has to choose between celebration and their observance.

Is there a dress code?

Yes — traditional. Chaniya choli, kediyu-dhoti, kurta or any Gujarati ethnic wear. It's not just aesthetic; it's the whole point.

Is the event family friendly?

Completely. It's an all-ages, alcohol-free traditional Garba with a reserved family seating zone, a women's help desk and on-site first aid.

Will there be Jain food options?

Yes. Jain and satvik counters will be clearly marked and prepared separately, with no root vegetables in the Jain stalls.

Do I need to bring my own dandiya sticks?

You don't have to — sticks are available at the venue, free with Couple and Parivaar passes and on request for Solo passes. Bring your own if you have a favourite pair.

How do I book a pass?

Bookings open with the final announcement. For now, fill the interest form on the Contact page — the interest list gets first access and early-bird pricing.
